This Statement of Work covers the National Standard engagement tier: re-instrumenting the existing Constituency Pulse platform onto live, national-scale data pipelines across all 543 Lok Sabha constituencies. Pilot and War Room variants are noted inline where terms differ — see §7.

1 Background

Constituency Pulse is a seat-level social and news intelligence platform for India's Lok Sabha elections, built against [PRD reference]. The current build is a fully working prototype: ten dashboard surfaces (overview, constituency explorer with electoral map, account explorer, leaderboard, issue tracking, growth alerts, influence network, war room, conversational assistant, and a public methodology page), running on deterministic mock data with real, cited election results and constituency boundaries underneath.

An earlier iteration of the prototype briefly ran live pipelines — YouTube and RSS ingestion, an LLM scoring pass, and a Postgres growth-snapshot store — against real, named channels. That was deliberately rolled back before production for reasons detailed in §6. This SOW is the engagement to rebuild those pipelines properly: at national scale, on licensed data access, with the compliance posture that rollback made necessary.

2 Scope of work

2.1 In scope

  • Ingestion pipelines for YouTube (Data API), vernacular news (RSS, scaling from 16 to 100+ feeds across India's 22 official languages), X/Twitter, and Instagram/Facebook via a licensed data-partner integration (Meta Content Library or equivalent).
  • Scheduled auto-refresh on all pipelines, target cadence 15–60 minutes on flagged/high-priority seats, daily nationally.
  • LLM analysis layer (sentiment, topic, and narrative extraction) reinstated against live content, issue-scored rather than person-scored per §6.
  • Persistence layer — Postgres, growth-snapshot history, alert-threshold storage, replacing the current mock-data layer without changing the front-end contracts the ten dashboard surfaces already consume.
  • National-scale hardening across all 543 constituencies: caching, rate-limit handling, and load testing on the dashboard, alerting, and network-graph surfaces.
  • Compliance layer: DPDP Act review, platform ToS review for each data source, and an updated public methodology page disclosing live-data sourcing.
  • Conversational assistant reconnected to live data (currently a deterministic mock-data router).
  • Knowledge transfer: architecture walkthrough and runbook for the client's engineering or ops team.

2.2 Out of scope

  • Paid data-partner licensing fees themselves (Meta Content Library or equivalent) — passed through per §5, not included in the build fee.
  • Paid API quota costs beyond the initial national rollout tier (YouTube Data API quota increases, LLM token costs beyond modeled volume) — billed as recurring cost per §5.
  • Campaign strategy, messaging, or creative services of any kind.
  • Legal representation before the Election Commission of India or any regulator — the compliance review in §2.1 is technical/architectural, not legal counsel.
  • Any tracking, scoring, or characterization of named private individuals — excluded by design, see §6.
  • Hosting/infrastructure costs beyond what's quoted in the retainer (client's own cloud billing account is assumed — see §8, Assumptions).

6 Data handling & compliance commitments

These commitments extend the design discipline already proven in the current prototype (see the prototype's internal build brief) into the live-data build:

  • No individual commenter data. Only aggregate counts (likes, comments, views) are stored — never a commenter's identity, even where a platform's API exposes it.
  • Issue-level scoring, never person-level. Sentiment, topic, and narrative analysis score the policy or issue under discussion. The system will not generate or store characterizations of a named individual's conduct or character.
  • Licensed access over scraping. Every ingestion pipeline uses an official or licensed API. No pipeline scrapes a platform outside its terms of service.
  • Public methodology disclosure. The live build retains and updates the existing methodology page, stating plainly which figures are sourced, modeled, or derived.
  • DPDP Act review. A named compliance pass (§2.1, §3 Phase 2) reviews the live-data build against India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act before any personal-adjacent data source goes live.
Why this section exists

An earlier version of this prototype briefly ran a live LLM call over real, named YouTube channels with a provisioned database. That was judged an unnecessary risk surface — platform ToS exposure, DPDP exposure, and reputational risk from characterizing real accounts — and was rolled back before production. This SOW does not repeat that shortcut: every live-data commitment above is a condition of delivery, not a best-effort aspiration.

8 Assumptions & dependencies

  • Client provides or funds a cloud hosting account (e.g. AWS/GCP/Neon) under the client's billing — vendor does not absorb infrastructure cost inside the fixed build fee.
  • Client secures or funds the Instagram/Facebook data-partner licensing agreement; vendor manages the technical integration only.
  • YouTube Data API and X/Twitter API access remain available on terms consistent with those in effect at signing. A material platform policy change affecting access is grounds for a change order.
  • Election-results and constituency-boundary data already in the prototype remain usable as-is; no new offline dataset licensing is assumed.
  • Client point of contact (§4) responds to milestone reviews within 5 business days; delays beyond that shift the timeline in §3 by the same duration.

9 Change orders, term & termination

Change orders. Any change to scope, tier, or the modeled volume assumptions in §5 is documented as a written change order, priced before work begins, and signed by both parties before it affects the timeline in §3.

Term. This SOW covers the build described in §3 plus the first month of the retainer described in §5. The retainer renews monthly thereafter until either party gives 30 days' written notice.

Termination. Either party may terminate for uncured material breach with 15 days' written notice to cure. On termination, the client pays for all milestones completed to date; the vendor delivers all work product completed to date within 10 business days.
