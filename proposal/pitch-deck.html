The problem

Campaigns don't lack data. They lack a seat-level nervous system.

Every national campaign already buys social listening. What none of it does is resolve down to the level that actually decides elections: this constituency, this week, these three creators moving the narrative — across YouTube, X, Instagram, Facebook and vernacular news, refreshed continuously rather than reported monthly.

Constituency Pulse is that layer. It's not a concept — it's a working platform, built end-to-end against a real PRD, with ten live dashboard surfaces already running. What we're proposing is the engagement to re-point that platform at live, national-scale data in time for the next cycle.

The pipeline, at national scale

Four sources in, one refreshed surface out.

Every platform feeds a scheduled ingestion layer, which hands raw content to an LLM pass for sentiment, topic, and narrative scoring, which lands in a growth-snapshot store — the same store the dashboards, alerts, and assistant all read from. The loop on the ingestion stage is what makes the platform feel live rather than reported-on.

Compliance — designed in, not bolted on

We already found the risk. We already built around it.

An earlier build of this prototype briefly tracked real, named YouTube channels behind a live LLM call and a provisioned database. We judged that an unnecessary risk surface for a demo — platform ToS exposure, India's DPDP Act constraints on scoring identifiable people, and reputational risk from characterizing real accounts — and rebuilt around it before this ever touched production. That discipline carries forward into the live rollout:

Licensed access, not scrapingInstagram / Facebook data comes through Meta's Content Library partner channel, not unauthorized scraping.
Aggregate signal onlyNo individual commenter identities — likes and comments are counted, never attributed to a person.
Issue-level scoring, never person-levelSentiment and narrative analysis score the policy or topic, never a person's character.
